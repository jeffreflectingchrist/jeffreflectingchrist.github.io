If someone asked you, "What do Christians mean by 'the gospel'?", how would you answer?

My Misunderstanding of the Gospel

I remember asking people when I was young: what exactly is the "gospel"?

The answer I got was: "The gospel is the good news that 'Jesus loves you,' 'God wants to bless you,' and 'we can go to heaven.'" Some people also told me, "The gospel is a very deep subject. You have to experience it with your life before you can understand its mysteries."

My understanding of the gospel also came from many testimonies. After people came to believe in Jesus, their lives changed for the better. For example, work that used to go badly led to a promotion and a raise once they believed. A troubled marriage became a happy family. Christianity made them better, kinder, and more capable.

So many success stories convinced me that the "gospel" meant this: God loves us. God wants to make my life better. God wants to give me a good job, good relationships, good health, and a better self. God wants me to have a happy, fulfilling life in this world. What exciting good news!

But my own life turned out very differently from the testimonies I had heard. My relationship with my parents felt like walking on thin ice. I hit wall after wall in dating. My face covered in acne, I was shunned and laughed at. My trust in God did not bring me the happy life I wanted.

I kept wondering: Is it because I don't trust God enough that my life still isn't blessed by him?

So what is the gospel, really?

The Gospel: What It Truly Means

After I understood what the Bible says, I realized I had misunderstood the gospel all along. I had always thought I was the main character of the gospel.

In fact, the gospel is the good news about Jesus Christ. The focus of the gospel is who Jesus is and what he has done.

The gospel is this good news: "Jesus Christ died for us as our Savior, and rose again for us as our King."

Where in the Bible can we draw this conclusion? 1 Corinthians 15 gives an excellent summary of the gospel.

(1 Corinthians 15:1–6, ESV)

First, the gospel is a message that "saves."

But this depends on our continuing to rely on it and hold fast to it. If we change the gospel or drift from it, we have believed in vain. Even if we have been baptized, go to church every week, and give generously, all of it is worthless if we do not hold firmly to the gospel. Only by trusting in the gospel alone can a person be "saved."

Second, the gospel is the message "of first importance."

It is the heart of the Bible. It must also be the heart of the church's message, continually shaping our lives and ministry. The gospel needs to permeate our preaching and our small groups and be the center of all our teaching. The gospel is the foundation of our hope and the basis of our good works.

Third, the gospel is the message about "Christ."

"Christ" (Greek) is the translation of "Messiah" (Hebrew), which literally means "the Anointed One," the king whom God has chosen. The Old Testament tells us that the Anointed One is the king God promised, who would come to save his people and rule over all nations forever (Psalm 2, 2 Samuel 7, Romans 1:4). So the Jesus who saves us is not only our Savior. He is also the King of our lives, the ruler we ought to obey.

Fourth, the gospel is the history of "Jesus' death and resurrection."

Jesus really died and was buried. He also really rose again, and the apostles and more than 500 others saw him with their own eyes and can bear witness to it.

Fifth, the gospel concerns the meaning of these historical events: that Christ died "for our sins."

Sin is not just bad behavior. Sin is our resistance to God's righteous rule over us, the attitude in our hearts that rejects God and wants to run our own lives. The wages of sin is death, followed by judgment after death (Hebrews 9:27). Yet the holy, sinless Christ willingly died for our sins. Jesus bore the punishment we deserved for rebelling against God. He died so that we could be forgiven and redeemed from God's wrath.

Sixth, the gospel is the message "according to the Scriptures."

The Scriptures point to the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. The whole Bible helps us understand what it truly means to say that Jesus is our Savior and King. Jesus himself taught this:

Click here, then paste the ESV text of Luke 24:25–27

(Luke 24:25–27, ESV)

Seventh, the gospel is a message that calls for our "response."

We need to respond by repenting and believing the gospel (Mark 1:14–15). "Faith" means believing and trusting: trusting that only Jesus can save us, and that no good works or other gods can save us. "Repentance" means a change of heart that leads to a change in behavior. It is a complete U-turn: from this moment on, we no longer live for ourselves but for God.

Finally, if we repent and turn to Christ, we will enjoy all the "blessings" of the gospel: redemption, eternal life, forgiveness of sins, becoming children of God, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, a deeper knowledge of God's love, and so much more.

All of these gospel blessings are better, more lasting, and more valuable than the worldly pleasures and blessings I had been chasing.

And You?

The gospel of Jesus Christ is the best news in the world. It is the good news that:

"Jesus Christ died for us as our Savior, and rose again for us as our King."

Today, if someone asked you what the gospel is, how would you answer? Whether or not you are a Christian, once we know the gospel, we all need to respond.

So will you choose to submit to Christ as your Savior and King?
